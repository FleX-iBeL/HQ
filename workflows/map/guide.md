# The map: the guide

For the agent who walks someone through their map (Pepper by default, or whoever they pick). This is how you do it, layer by layer. Their own map is `map.md` in their workspace; what they say goes there, in their words.

The map is an upside-down pyramid. On top, wide: the life they want three to five years from now. Under it, each layer narrower and closer: the year, the ideal week, the real week. At the bottom: looking back, which feeds every layer again. Each layer is checked against the one above it.

1. The vision (three to five years out): the why, where it's all going.
2. The year: the handful of big things the next twelve months need to bring.
3. The ideal week: the shape of a good week, a template.
4. The real week: this week, poured into that template.
5. Looking back: every day a little, every week properly; what changes.

This is a big piece of work and it's worth the time. Never rush it and never skip a layer's questions to get to the end. One sitting is one layer, or part of one; most people need four or five sittings for the first time through, and that's fine.

## How you go through it

**Before the first question.** Read their `map.md` and anything it points to. Find the first layer that isn't done (each layer has a `Status:` line). Tell them in a sentence where they are ("We did the vision last time; the year is next") and ask if they want to carry on there. If they'd rather start somewhere else, go there; if that layer leans on one above it that's still empty, say so once, then follow them.

**One question at a time.** Ask one, wait, listen. Never a list of questions in one go, and on a call never more than one question per turn. The questions under each layer are a route, not a script: say them in your own words, skip one they've already answered, and follow up when an answer is thin.

**Spar, don't interview.** You're a sparring partner, not a form.
- Ask for the gut answer first: "Don't think too hard, what comes up first?" The polish comes later.
- Pull vague into specific: names, places, numbers, times, what it looks like on an ordinary Tuesday. "More freedom" becomes "which days, doing what, with whom".
- Reflect back what you heard, short, in their words, and ask if that's it.
- Notice what lights them up and what they sigh at; name it once, plainly.
- When an answer sounds like somebody else's wish (a parent's, a partner's, what looks good), ask gently whether it's really theirs.
- Don't advise unasked. If they want your view, give two or three options and let them choose.
- Keep it honest without blame. When something didn't work, the useful question is what was in their control, because that's what they can change.
- When they're tired or stuck, stop. Write down where you are and pick it up next time.

**Write it down as you go.** After each answer, write it into their `map.md`, under the layer you're on: the question in a few words, then their answer in their words (fix obvious dictation slips, don't paraphrase, don't condense). When you add something of your own, a suggestion or a summary, mark it as yours in [square brackets]. Update the layer's `Status:` line as you go: `in progress (up to: <the last question>)`, and `done (<date>)` once the layer's result is written and they've said yes to it. Then a quick conversation can stop at any moment and nothing is lost.

**Close each layer.** When the questions are through: write the layer's result (what each layer below says it makes), read it back to them in a few sentences, let them correct it, and only then mark it done. Then say what goes down to the next layer.

**Never say where it comes from.** These are just the questions; no names of people, books, courses or programmes, not here and not to them.

**Their vision is theirs.** The vision only changes when they say so. The lower layers you adjust together whenever looking back says they should.

## 1. The vision: three to five years out

**What it is.** The life they want, three to five years from now, as clear as if they're already living it. Not goals yet, not a plan: the picture everything below gets checked against. It answers why.

**Before you start.** If they already have something (an earlier vision, a list of goals, a questionnaire they filled in, a vision board, notes), ask for it and start from there: read it, keep it word for word in their workspace, and only ask the questions below that it doesn't answer. If their `map.md` says the vision lives in other files, read those; don't do it again.

**How to ask.** Have them answer as their future self, in the first person and the present tense: "I wake up at…", not "I'd like to…". Gut answers, specifics over polish, as many words as they like.

**The questions,** roughly in this order:

The shape of it
1. What's this chapter of your life about? What are you building or becoming in it?
2. Three to five years from now, on the best version of how it goes: what's true?
3. Walk me through an ordinary good day in that life, from waking up to falling asleep. Where are you, who's there, what do you do first?
4. And a great day at work in that life: what happens, who's in the room, how does it end?

Work and money
5. What work fills your days, and what does it never include anymore?
6. How many days a week and how many hours do you work? From where?
7. What comes in each month, and what's in the bank? Any debts left?
8. What are the people you work with saying about you and your work?
9. Is there a team around you? What's it like for them?

People
10. Who are the most important people in that life, and how much time do you spend with each?
11. How do you show up for your family, your partner, your friends? What can you give them that you can't now?
12. Who are you surrounded by, and what do they have in common?

Body and mind
13. How does your body feel? What's your energy like, your health, your sleep?
14. How do you move every day? How do you eat?
15. How do you look after your head: rest, offline time, what you read, what you play?

Places, things and experiences
16. Where do you live, and where do you travel to? Describe your home, and the place you work from.
17. What are you doing that's new, the experiences you'd love to have had by then?
18. Which things would you love to own, and which don't matter to you at all?

Who you are
19. What kind of person do you have to be to live this? What do you do easily that's hard for you now?
20. Which habits are gone? Which ones got you there?
21. What did you have to get through or let go of on the way?
22. If that future you could send one piece of advice back to you today, what would it be?

**Check it with them,** one at a time and briefly:
- Is this really yours, or partly what somebody else wants for you?
- Is it specific enough to picture? Where's it still vague?
- Does it excite you, and scare you a little? If it doesn't, is it big enough?
- Is there anything in there you'd actually be relieved to drop?

**What it makes.** In their `map.md` (or the file it points to), with their yes:
- **A day in that life:** one day, written from their answers in the present tense, with their real details (places, people, numbers, small habits), told like it's happening.
- **The deeper truth:** a short paragraph on what made that life possible: the mindset under it.
- **A motto:** one line they'd say to themselves on a hard day. Theirs, or from their own words.
- **Action steps:** three to six things they can start doing now that move them towards it, each small and concrete.

**What it hands down.** To the year: the action steps, the three or four areas of life that matter most in the vision, and the numbers that are in it (what comes in, the days off, the hours worked).

## 2. The year

**What it is.** What the next twelve months need to bring so the vision gets closer. A handful of big things spread over the months, not a to-do list. The order matters: rest and the things that make life good go in first, then the work that earns, then everything else fits around them. A year planned that way gets bigger work and a better life; a year planned the other way gets a full calendar and life gets the leftovers.

**Before you start.** Read the vision layer and its action steps. Have a year calendar at hand (their own, or HQ's calendar), even a rough one: a lot of this layer ends up in it.

**The questions,** in this order:

Looking back first
1. What are three wins or highlights from the past months? Not only achievements: good moments count.
2. What did you set out to do this year? Where are you on it: ahead, on track, behind?
3. How hard did you really go for it, from one to ten? And how did the results come out, one to ten?
4. If the effort and the results don't match, why do you think that is? Be straight about it: which part was in your hands?
5. What are you going to stop doing? Three to five things you'll leave behind, including what you tried and know isn't for you.
6. What worked, that you'll keep doing?
7. What's new, the next step of you?

Life first
8. What one challenge would make this year the one you talk about later: "This was the year I…"? Hard, exciting, a little scary. Business or personal, either is fine.
9. What smaller adventures do you want this year, about one every two months: a trip, a race, a course, a concert, something with your people?
10. How much time off do you want: each week, each month, each quarter, once in the year? Where, doing what, with whom?
11. If that challenge, those adventures and that time off all happened, whatever happened at work, how would this year be?

Then the money
12. Where are you now: what comes in each month, how many clients or customers, how many truly free days a month?
13. Twelve months from now, what do you want those three numbers to be?
14. Of everything you did to earn this year, what actually moved the needle? What worked best, even a little?
15. What are the one or two things you'll lean on to earn next year, and how often does each need to happen to get to your numbers?
16. What do you deliver to the people who pay you, and at what rhythm?

Goals
17. Three goals for the year (or the next six months), each one you can say yes or no to on a date: "by 1 March I have…", not "more money" or "be healthier".
18. For each goal: what are the first three steps?
19. What could you do every day or every week that would make it almost impossible to miss that goal?
20. What will you do in the next thirty days to get going?

Habits
21. What's the one habit that holds you back most right now? One, not seven.
22. What's the one habit you'll start, and how do you set up your surroundings so it's easy to win? (Willpower alone doesn't hold.)

The stress test
23. If you live this year as it's planned now, do you get to your numbers and your goals? If not, back to the earning part.
24. And if you live it, does anything break: your health, your relationship, your sleep? If yes, take pieces out.
25. Played out like this, how does the year end?
26. Where might you trip up or get overwhelmed? What's not clear yet, and where do you need help?

Then let it sit. Suggest they take it somewhere they think well, talk it through with their partner and the people it touches (holidays, trips, time away), and come back to it before it's final.

**What it makes.** In `map.md`, with their yes:
- **The year at a glance:** month by month, the big things: the challenge, the adventures, the time off, the earning activities and their rhythm, deadlines that matter.
- **Three goals,** each with a date, its first three steps and the thirty-day start.
- **The habit to stop and the habit to start,** with how the surroundings support it.
- Optionally: the stop, keep and start lists.

Offer to put the fixed parts into their calendar (time off, the adventures, the earning rhythm); only with their yes.

**What it hands down.** To the ideal week: the time off and recovery that are fixed, the earning activities and how often they happen, the goals' weekly actions, and the habit pair.

## 3. The ideal week

**What it is.** The shape of a good week, as a template the real weeks are poured into: when they work and for how long, when they recharge, the people, the movement, sleep and waking up, the offline time, time to steer. Built around their energy first and the results second. It's an aim, not a cage: hitting it most weeks, about four out of five days going as planned, is a win.

**Before you start.** Read the year and its hand-down. A blank week grid helps (on paper, or in words: day by day, morning, afternoon, evening).

**The questions,** in this order:

Where the time goes now
1. Think back over the last seven days, or look at your calendar and your phone's screen time: what are about five things you spent your time on each day, and roughly how long?
2. For each: does it earn, or move earning closer? And did it give you energy or take it?
3. Of what doesn't earn and drains you: what can simply go, what could be automated, what could someone else do? What do you want to keep because it's yours?

Your energy
4. When in the day are you sharpest? When do you dip?
5. What fills you up again: what has to be in the week for you to feel good?
6. When do you want to wake up and go to sleep, every day of the week? What do the hours before sleep look like?

The ingredients
7. List everything that would make your week great. Go kind by kind:
   - **Recharge:** movement, rest, people, play, being outside, being offline.
   - **Earning:** the work that brings money in (from the year).
   - **The work itself:** what you deliver for the people who pay you.
   - **Admin:** mail, messages, bills, scheduling, the house.
   - **Steering:** time on your life and work instead of in it: planning, learning, looking back, deciding.
   Where something fits two kinds, they choose by how it feels to them.
8. How many days do you work, and how many hours on each? Could it be fewer, in tighter blocks? (Work tends to fill whatever time it's given.)

Placing it
9. Recharge and earning go in first. Where in the week do they go, on which days and at what times?
10. Where does the work itself go, in which fixed windows, so it doesn't spill over everything?
11. When's admin? One block a day, so it doesn't leak through the whole day.
12. Where are a few hours of steering, including a fixed moment to plan the coming week?
13. Do some days get a theme (one kind of work per day), so you switch less? Which days are fully off?
14. Where's the margin, the empty space for when a day goes differently?

The check
15. Looking at the whole week: does it fit the year, and does it look like the life in the vision, already a little?
16. Is it too full? What would you take out first?

**What it makes.** In `map.md`, with their yes:
- **The week, day by day:** waking up and bedtime, the blocks in order with their times and their kind, which days are off.
- **The rules of the week** in a few lines: work hours, the offline times, the admin block, when the week is planned.
- **The keep, drop, automate and hand-off list** from the first questions, with what to do about each.

Offer to set the template up as recurring blocks in their calendar; only with their yes. Things to automate or hand off can go on their to-do list.

**What it hands down.** To the real week: the template itself, the fixed moment the week is planned, and the weekly part of the year's goals.

## 4. The real week

**What it is.** This week, in the calendar and on the to-do list, poured into the ideal week. The template meets real life: appointments, deadlines, the things that came up. Important but not urgent goes first, because that's where the vision lives; the rest fits around it. A deadline gets a slot well before its date.

**When.** At the fixed planning moment from the ideal week, about thirty to sixty minutes, ideally relaxed. Start each time from looking back (layer 5) at last week.

**The questions,** each week:
1. What's already fixed this week: appointments, other people, travel?
2. Where does that bend the ideal week, and how do we bend it back as close as we can?
3. What are your three focuses this week: the three things that, if they're done, make it a good week? Do they serve the year's goals?
4. On the to-do list: what's important but not urgent? Which of those gets a slot this week?
5. Which deadlines are coming, and where's their slot, well before the date?
6. Is recovery in first: sleep, movement, the people, the time off?
7. What's this week saying no to?
8. Looking at the whole week: does it pull away from the vision anywhere? (If it does, say so once, plainly, and let them decide.)

**What it makes.** The week in their calendar (on their yes) and the three focuses in `map.md`, under this layer, with the week's date.

**What it hands down.** To looking back: the week as planned, so next time what happened can be held up against it.

## 5. Looking back

**What it is.** The step that keeps the map alive. Each day a short look, each week a proper one; now and then a bigger one. This is where the ideal week and the year get adjusted, so the plan learns. The vision only changes when they say so.

**Each day,** two or three minutes, in the evening:
1. What got done today? What are you glad about?
2. What didn't happen as planned, and why, in one line?
3. What's the one thing tomorrow is for?

**Each week,** before planning the real week, thirty minutes or so:
1. What were the wins last week? What are you proud of?
2. What didn't get done, or got skipped? (If they tick off and cross out their calendar, use that: what's ticked, what got an X, what's not marked.)
3. Is there a pattern? Which kind of block keeps slipping, on which day, at what time?
4. Effort and results this week, one to ten each. If they're far apart, why?
5. What will you change: in the ideal week (a block moved, made smaller, dropped), in how you plan, or in the year?
6. Then on to the real week (layer 4) with the three focuses.

**Each quarter or half year,** an hour: go back through the year's questions 1 to 7 (wins, the goals, effort and results, stop, keep, start) and the stress test, and adjust the year. Give a new approach about three honest tries before you change it; and ask of everything in the plan: is it working, and does it work for me, for who I am and the season I'm in?

**Once a year,** read the vision together. Is it still theirs? Only they decide if it changes.

**What it makes.** Short notes per look under this layer in `map.md` (date, what came out, what changes), and the changes themselves in the layer they belong to, on their yes.

**What it hands back up.** The changes to the ideal week and the year, and, only when they say so, to the vision.

## In short

Vision: the why, a day in that life, the deeper truth, a motto, a few action steps. The year: look back, life first, then the money, three dated goals, one habit out and one in, the stress test. The ideal week: where the time goes, their energy, the ingredients in five kinds, recharge and earning first, margin. The real week: the template meets this week, three focuses, important but not urgent first. Looking back: daily short, weekly proper, and it changes the layers above.
